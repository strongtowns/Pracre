---

Pracre 1.1.0 reads every coordinate system, more location formats and more kinds of assessment file, folds strata units drawn in holes of their common property into their lot, and ships the whole Township of Langley and Port Huron, Michigan as samples.

This round comes from testing against open parcel and assessment data from Vancouver, Edmonton, Calgary, New York City, Philadelphia, Wake County (North Carolina), Massachusetts and France.

**Every coordinate system is built in.** All 7,800 EPSG and ESRI projected systems and 1,400 geographic ones, from PROJ's database, so a bare code such as EPSG:2264 or an ArcGIS wkid works offline and State Plane files from ArcGIS land in the right place without a .prj. The methods parcel data uses are all there, Krovak, the Swiss and Dutch grids, oblique and polar stereographic, Cassini, Polyconic and Lambert azimuthal among them, with axes stored southing or westing first read the right way round. Against PROJ at about 39,000 points every method agrees to a centimetre, the Swiss grids to 4 cm. About 38 systems on rarer methods (Equal Earth, the New Zealand Map Grid, Krovak Modified) say they are unsupported rather than guess.

**Older datums shift to WGS 84 for where the data sits.** NAD27, OSGB36, ED50, MGI, Tokyo and the rest take the regional Helmert or offset transformation whose area covers the data, and 7,550 of 7,740 systems land within 2 m of PROJ end to end. NAD27 is good to 5 to 10 m, since grid shifts are not included. WKT2 text from epsg.io works, as do .prj files that name only their datum.

**Unlabelled coordinates find their system.** They are tried in every projected system near the map view, and the import window shows where they land. "Guess for This Area" ranks the systems again for wherever its small map is moved, which settles State Plane files that fit many states.

**More location formats.** MGRS and USNG grid references, UTM text such as "10U 525199 5432031", Plus codes and geohashes in a column; hex WKB geometry, as in Philadelphia's assessment CSV and other PostGIS or Carto exports; Opendatasoft JSON, UTF-16 text from Excel, decimal commas in semicolon-separated files and CSV files with title rows above the header.

**Strata units in holes of their common property join their lot.** In the Township of Langley the 182 townhouse units at 20391 96 AVE sit in 34 holes cut out of the common property at 20381 96 AVE. They stood alone at $83,600 of tax per acre and now fold into one 17.6 acre lot at about $29,600, with their land and shapes added. A parcel counts as common property only with an address or name, no value of its own and at least two units in its holes, so road right-of-way shapes (3,689 lots sit in Abington's, in the MassGIS data) and assessed lots keep the parcels they surround. Condo master parcels in Port Huron and HOA lots in Raleigh fold the same way.

**Values link more often.** Multi-year exports, such as Vancouver's tax report, use only the latest roll year instead of adding the years together. Tables that repeat a lot's ID for every strata unit link by that ID, so condo units reach their lot. current_land_value, county values and taxable land plus buildings read as values, and ID, legal description and building count columns no longer read as values or locations. Dropping all of a shapefile's parts at once imports it once, and empty files and ArcGIS error replies say what is wrong.

**Two samples under File > Sample Data.** The whole Township of Langley, 53,199 parcels and 49,507 assessment records with its 2024 tax rates ($231.25M of tax), and every parcel in Port Huron, Michigan with its 2024 taxable value (12,346 parcels, $664M), without owner names or mailing addresses. A sample opens in place of what is loaded, after asking about unsaved changes.

**The summary card is shorter, and can be hidden.** It shows the map title, total, acres and parcel count, and Display > Map > Show summary card turns it off. The card, image and web map titles follow the measure on the map (value or tax, per acre or per hectare), exported images and web maps leave out the half-of-revenue line too, and a sample's title names the measure it opens with.

**Fixes in the app.** Opening a project restores its display settings and camera. The Tax Rates table keeps rows and units edited while a save is in progress, deleted rows disappear at once and rates typed with a decimal comma read correctly. The map moves to the data when a dataset's location changes, and switching to 3D no longer cuts short a zoom to the data. The import window's small map stays in place while you edit, with zoom buttons, and marking a column as a tax rate no longer breaks the window. The checklist explains when a values file covers more ground than the parcels and points to the link settings when no values reach the map. CSV exports can no longer carry spreadsheet formulas from imported text.

**Windows and links.** Windows installs into Program Files\STCD\Pracre. The About dialog and Help menu link to strongtowns.dev, and the toolbar shows the name alone beside the logo.

**Checks.** CI on 05f8e4c passed every leg, covering the tests on Linux, the Windows, macOS and Linux builds and the release. `go test` covers the projections, the EPSG table, the location encodings, the common property merge and both samples, and with the Township files beside the repository runs the whole pipeline to the Township's $231,250,988 tax total.
